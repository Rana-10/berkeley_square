# Open editorial layouts beat cards for UK property sites

UK property and property-finance sites share a verifiable structural skeleton: a utility bar with a valuation or enquiry call to action, a five-to-six item mega-menu, a hero, service or sector tiles, a proof strip of years and figures, an insight feed, and a footer dense with regulatory text. The visual layer is another matter. The research fetched text only, so **no font, hex value, grid or crop ratio was verified for any firm**. Everything about typography, palette and photography style below is labelled as inference, drawn from design practice rather than from the sites. The incumbents mostly use card grids, so a no-text-in-card site is a deliberate departure and not an imitation. The departure is workable because the data the sites show (image, title, beds, guide price, amount, term, LTV, sector label) maps cleanly onto captions beneath images, ruled rows and key-facts lists. For a home, loans, property, construction, services, gallery and contact site, the recommended system is a cream ground with a single deep green or navy section tone and a muted accent. It sets one serif for headings and body, with a small-caps sans for labels, and uses hairline-ruled index lists, definition-list key facts and full-bleed architectural bands captioned below. It carries the full set of UK regulatory and credential text in the footer and on finance pages. Compliance wording needs review by the client's compliance adviser.

## What the verified record shows: a shared skeleton, tiles for entry, and heritage proof

**Structure is the well-evidenced part.** Savills has a header with valuation booking, search and account, six menu items, and sentence-case headings. Its body runs from a testimonial banner to service pillars, a valuation call to action, a stats block and an insight feed ([Savills](https://www.savills.co.uk)). Knight Frank opens with a full-width house-on-a-hill hero, then a carousel of tiles named for property types (New homes, Coastal, Farm houses, Period homes, Mews, Thameside), five mega-menu groups, five audience service cards, a stats block and dated insights ([Knight Frank](https://www.knightfrank.co.uk)). Carter Jonas uses six image sector tiles with "View sector" links and a footer "Credentials" group ([Carter Jonas](https://www.carterjonas.co.uk)). Strutt & Parker shows eight featured listings, each with photo, title, bedrooms, a short description and guide price ([Strutt & Parker](https://www.struttandparker.com)). Entry by lifestyle or architectural type, rather than by form fields, is a recurring editorial device. Search is either embedded in the hero (Knight Frank, Strutt & Parker, Carter Jonas) or sits behind the navigation (Savills, which shows no listings on its homepage) ([Savills](https://www.savills.com/)). Dexters adds curated collections such as Historic London Houses and Prime Central London as entry points ([Dexters](https://www.dexters.co.uk/)). Cluttons, Jackson-Stops, Hamptons and the Chestertons UK site returned 403 or 404, so their structure is unverified.

**Navigation and headers.** Verified items are the valuation call to action, an account link and a short mega-menu. Foxtons groups "Help for Landlords", "Specialist Lets" and "Legal & Compliance" in its navigation, and Winkworth offers Buy, Sell, Rent and Landlords cards ([Foxtons](https://www.foxtons.co.uk/); [Winkworth](https://www.winkworth.co.uk/)). Finance firms route by audience: Hampshire Trust Bank splits Personal, Business and Intermediaries ([HTB](https://www.htb.co.uk/)). Together has a "for myself / on behalf of a client" selector ([Together](https://www.togethermoney.com)). Pure Property Finance has a mega-menu by Bridging, Development, Commercial, Residential, Secured and Insurance ([Pure Property Finance](https://www.purepropertyfinance.co.uk)). Putting a phone number with hours in the header is an inference from Dexters' published 8am to 8pm, seven-day line ([Dexters](https://www.dexters.co.uk/)).

**Trust signals.** The heritage date and scale figure is near-universal: Savills cites 170+ years ([Savills](https://www.savills.co.uk)). Knight Frank cites 130 years and 600+ offices ([Knight Frank](https://www.knightfrank.co.uk)). Carter Jonas cites 1855, a 4.7 Google rating and credential logos for RICS, RTPI, The Property Ombudsman and ARLA ([Carter Jonas](https://www.carterjonas.co.uk)). Strutt & Parker's 2026 rebrand adopts "Land and property. Since 1885." ([S&P press release](https://www.struttandparker.com/about-strutt-parker/corporate-news/strutt-and-parker-unveils-significant-rebrand)). Named, dated reviews appear at Winkworth, and Foxtons shows 4.50 from 8,756 reviews ([Winkworth](https://www.winkworth.co.uk/); [Foxtons](https://www.foxtons.co.uk/)). Consistency matters: S&P's copy says both "over 135 years" and "over 140 years", and Pure's lender count appears three ways, which the research flags as credibility damage ([Strutt & Parker](https://www.struttandparker.com); [Pure](https://www.purepropertyfinance.co.uk)). Each figure should appear once, in one form.

## Listings and galleries: image first, text beneath, floorplan as a peer

Rightmove cards place the photo first, then price (with qualifiers such as "Guide Price"), address, type, bedroom-style figures, a short summary and agent attribution, so text sits below the photo rather than over it ([Rightmove](https://www.rightmove.co.uk/property-for-sale/find.html?locationIdentifier=REGION%5E87490)). Its 2020 detail redesign moved photos above price and address, replaced tabs with scrolling sections, folded video into the gallery, kept original photo proportions, and gave floorplans their own full-screen gallery one click from the photos ([Patma](https://www.patma.co.uk/blog/2020/10/the-new-rightmove-page-layout/); [Rightmove hub](https://hub.rightmove.co.uk/your-new-rightmove-overseas-property-details-page/)). This evidence is from 2020 and may be dated. Chestertons lists price, beds, baths and area per featured property ([Chestertons](https://www.chestertons.com)). Knight Frank's feature blocks overlay copy on full-width photographs, with descriptive alt text on content images ([Knight Frank](https://www.knightfrank.com/)).

Two points are inference, not observation. First, the notes describe a "ruled list of key facts" as a natural rendering of the portals' fact rows, not as their literal markup. Second, **no fetched site was shown to use hairline-ruled rows, numbered rows, or captions beneath images in a rendered view**; this remains unverified for every firm. The sourced part is the data model and the image-first order. The editorial treatment is ours to design. In practice that means the property page should lead with a large photograph, follow with price and address as plain type, then a definition list (beds, baths, receptions, size, tenure, EPC, council tax band), then a floorplan as a labelled section or toggle, and a lightbox that preserves aspect ratio. The gallery page should follow the same logic with captions below and an architecture-type taxonomy (Georgian terrace, stucco, brick, mews, country estate), echoing Knight Frank's labelled tiles. Team and agent page layouts were not researched, so any people section is unsupported by this evidence.

## Typography, colour and photography are inferred, with thin sourcing

**What is sourced.** Savills uses a lowercase bold sans wordmark in yellow and red (hex values from a logo site, not official), so bright palettes exist in the sector ([logotyp.us](https://logotyp.us/logo/savills/)). Knight Frank kept classic green and red through its 1996 identity change ([Design Week](https://www.designweek.co.uk/issues/11-january-1996/estate-agent-converts-logo/)). Carter Jonas was historically red and green, with a 2014 refresh ([Carter Jonas 2014](https://carterjonas.co.uk/news-and-events/archive/sep-14/brand-refresh-press-release)). Cluttons' 2014 identity used a wide-spaced white wordmark and avoided green because Foxtons owns it ([Communicate Magazine](https://www.communicatemagazine.com/news/2014/1402-property-rebrand-evokes-luxury-and-ease)); that source is old. The clearest current signal is Strutt & Parker's 2026 move to a deep claret, a "refined, balanced, monochromatic" palette and a "discerning and timeless" feel, with the red box removed ([S&P press release](https://www.struttandparker.com/about-strutt-parker/corporate-news/strutt-and-parker-unveils-significant-rebrand); [Estate Agent Today](https://www.estateagenttoday.co.uk/breaking-news/2026/04/historic-estate-agency-unveils-modern-rebrand/)). The direction of travel is from bright corporate accents to restrained single-hue palettes. Farrow & Ball's deep navies, dusky pinks and warm off-whites show British heritage colour culture, but the hex values in that source are unofficial ([Made Good Designs](https://madegooddesigns.com/farrow-ball-brand-guidelines/)). Sentence-case headings are confirmed only at Savills. Typefaces are unverified for every firm; Georgian signage conventions (hand-painted fascia lettering, gilded lettering, muted small-scale conservation guidance) are the heritage reference ([Designing Buildings](https://www.designingbuildings.co.uk/wiki/Shop%20signs)).

**Inferred recommendations (not verified against any competitor).** Use one Garamond, Caslon or Baskerville-lineage serif for headings and body (for example Cormorant Garamond, EB Garamond or Libre Baskerville), with a restrained sans only for small-caps labels, navigation and figures. Typewolf lists Cormorant as a Garamond-inspired display face, and pairs Proza Libre with Cormorant and Libre Franklin with Libre Baskerville ([Typewolf Cormorant](https://www.typewolf.com/cormorant); [Typewolf pairings](https://www.typewolf.com/blog/google-fonts-combinations)); the pairings post is undated. Keep display serifs large and avoid them for small labels. Track capitals at about 0.12 to 0.2em at 11 to 13px for labels. Use lining tabular numerals for financial data. For colour, use a stone or cream ground (around #F4F0E8), warm near-black ink, one deep green or navy as the anchor tone, one burgundy accent, and brass only for hairlines because brass on cream typically fails 4.5:1 for text; alternate cream and deep-green sections for rhythm. These hex ranges come from the notes' own synthesis and need contrast testing.

**Photography and architecture.** The sourced architecture facts are the vocabulary: Belgravia's white stucco, porticoes and garden squares, with the Grosvenor estate dictating window heights and railings, and Mayfair's grid of broad streets and mews ([British History Online](https://prod.british-history.ac.uk/node/34900)); Mayfair House pairs matching stucco with Georgian-proportioned windows and a Cotswold Buff brick rear ([Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2013/10/07/mayfair-house-by-squire-and-partners/)). The photographic craft rule is to keep the sensor parallel to the facade and fix height by viewpoint, with wide angles worsening distortion ([Digifotostarter](https://www.digifotostarter.nl/en/architectural-photography-27-the-art-of-the-straight-line); [Luxury Real Estate Images](https://luxuryrealestateimages.com/5-tips-getting-verticals-straight-photographing-real-estate)). Everything else is inferred: a shot list led by a black door with fanlight (portrait crop), a stucco terrace at dusk, a red-brick Victorian bay, railings and area steps, sash windows, and a Cotswold stone group for country work; at most one skyline; no fisheye or heavy HDR; one consistent warm grade; images that read as one real place instead of stock variety. Avoid US brownstones and glass condo towers because UK viewers notice. Whether any incumbent actually crops or grades this way could not be checked.

## No-card layout principles, mostly inferred, with a sourced contrast and image-handling floor

Editorial showcase pages confirm hairline-led, fill-free structures: hairline rules with heavier rules under table headers, numbered "Section 01" headings, and roomy ruled data rows ([getdesign.md](https://getdesign.md/design-md/wired/preview); [SuperDesign](https://superdesign.dev/library/the-editorial-data-table); [shadcn.io](https://www.shadcn.io/design/the-guardian/raw)). These are design showcases, not authoritative research, and the notes say so. Our twelve devices are inferences from practice. Ruled index rows (number, title, one-line descriptor, right-aligned meta, whole row a link) replace service and property cards. Numbered sections and tracked small-caps eyebrows replace pill badges. Asymmetric splits (about 4/8 or 5/7) with a narrow label column replace mirrored image-and-text zig-zags. Full-bleed bands (21:9 or 16:7) carry captions below, such as "Eaton Square, 1840s stucco". Definition lists replace stat cards. Pull quotes are large serif italic with a hairline above and no icons. Add accordion rows with hairline separators for FAQs, and a ledger with year in the left column for track record.

On text over photography, the sourced rule is WCAG 1.4.3: 4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large text (24px, or 18.66px bold), measured against the background, with insufficient-contrast background images a documented failure ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html)). The preferred method, which is inference, is no overlay: put headings in the section above or below the image, or place display type in a calm sky or plain-stucco zone, using a CSS tint of 25 to 40% in the ink colour or a bottom-up gradient only if needed. Keep body copy off images and avoid frosted-glass panels, which are boxes in disguise. Check `object-position` at several viewports, since crops move what sits behind the type. For implementation, the Next.js Image docs confirm that width and height reserve space, `fill` with `objectFit: cover` suits unknown ratios, `alt` is required, and in Next 16 `priority` is deprecated in favour of `preload` or `fetchPriority="high"` ([Next.js docs](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/api-reference/components/image)). Apply `sizes` that mirror the CSS columns and limit eager loading to the single LCP hero (practice, unverified in the notes). Use at most three image ratios per page (3:2 default, 4:5 portrait detail, 21:9 band), with the same ratio inside any one ruled list. Hover should be slow (400 to 600ms) and subtle, respecting `prefers-reduced-motion`. Parallax, if used at all, stays under 5 to 10% and is off on mobile. All of this is unsourced beyond the WCAG and Next.js points.

## Page-by-page application, regulatory text, and the generic-site tells to avoid

| Page | Open editorial treatment | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Home | Single calm hero, plain type below or in a clear zone; one-line intro; ruled index to loans, property, construction, services; one stats ledger; one quiet "Make an enquiry" | Skeleton sourced (Savills, Knight Frank, S&P); treatment inferred |
| Loans | Per-product ruled table or definition list (amount, term, LTV, rate "from" with indicative qualifier); 4 to 5 numbered process steps with timings; case-study rows with asset image, amount, term, LTV, location, date | Cohort, Octane, Ortus data model sourced; table and numbering inferred |
| Property | Photo, price and address as plain type, definition-list key facts, floorplan as peer section, aspect-preserving lightbox | Rightmove order sourced; ruled rendering inferred |
| Construction | Project index as numbered rows with image, stage, location, year; ledger of completions | Inferred, analogous to Cohort transactions and Ortus dated case studies |
| Services | Audience-routed index (landlord, tenant, freeholder, borrower, broker) as ruled rows | Audience routing sourced (Winkworth, Knight Frank, HTB, Together); rows inferred |
| Gallery | Taxonomy by architectural type, captions beneath, no overlays, consistent grade | Labels sourced (Knight Frank); rest inferred |
| Contact | Plain-text address block, named contact, phone with hours, short form (loan amount, security location, purpose, timescale), registered details | Phone hours (Dexters) sourced; form fields and letterhead layout inferred |

**Loan data and CTA.** None of the fetched lender homepages used a full rate, LTV and term table. Cohort puts amount, term and LTV on each transaction (for example NoMad Hotel Covent Garden £73m, 24 months, 70%, and Chilham Castle, Kent, £9.1m, 18 months, 60%) ([Cohort Capital](https://cohortcapital.com)). Octane shows "from" rates such as 0.73% pm for bridging ([Octane Capital](https://www.octanecapital.co.uk)). Ortus gives a 6 to 24 month term and a credit-backed agreement in principle within 24 hours ([Ortus](https://www.ortussecuredfinance.co.uk)). A ruled per-product table is therefore under-used, not conventional. Competitors use repeated verb calls to action ("Loan Enquiry", "Apply now", "Get a free quote"); a single quiet repeated enquiry link is the proposed private-bank register (inference). Named, titled testimonials read as more credible than anonymous ones (Cohort versus Octane; the credibility judgement is inference).

**Footers and regulation.** Wording depends on the client's status, so match reality and get compliance review. Sourced patterns: Cohort states it is not FCA authorised, bridging is not regulated investment business, and no FSCS eligibility ([Cohort Capital](https://cohortcapital.com)). Octane states it is a trading name of Aldermore Bank PLC and notes some lending is unregulated ([Octane Capital](https://www.octanecapital.co.uk)). HTB lists company number, registered office and FCA and PRA reference numbers ([HTB](https://www.htb.co.uk/)). Pure, a broker, adds a repossession warning, commission disclosure, ICO registration and NACFB membership ([Pure](https://www.purepropertyfinance.co.uk)). Savills carries an FCA partial-regulation statement ([Savills](https://www.savills.com/)), and Knight Frank links client money protection and fraud protection in its governance footer column ([Knight Frank](https://www.knightfrank.com/)). Dexters lists complaints, cookie, modern slavery and tax strategy links ([Dexters](https://www.dexters.co.uk/)). For lettings or management, the law requires publishing fee lists with client money protection and redress scheme statements, with certificates available ([legislation.gov.uk s.83](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/section/83/data.htm); [Business Companion](https://www.businesscompanion.info/en/quick-guides/services/letting-agents-display-of-fees)). That guide has a Renters' Rights Act 2025 section, so rules may have shifted; verify against live GOV.UK. A fees page as a plain ruled table next to those statements is our inference. The footer set (registered office, company number, relevant memberships, complaints procedure, privacy and cookies, modern slavery) is sector knowledge plus these sources, not an audit of each site.

**Avoiding the generic look.** Sources agree AI-built sites converge on Inter, gradients, a centred hero and three equal cards because of averaged training data and shared stacks ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/lyd_lyd_d4869af25d33ce0eb/why-do-ai-generated-websites-all-look-the-same-k2j); [SuperDesign](https://superdesign.dev/blog/why-ai-design-looks-generic)). These are vendor blogs with a commercial interest, so treat the diagnosis as plausible and the remedies as self-serving. The tells listed in the notes (icon cards, pill badges, glow, a row of generic counters, handshake stock, carousel testimonials) and the UK-specific errors (US spellings, missing registration detail, no named people or street address) are inferences. They align with the card-free brief: real addresses, dates, named people and a consistent ruled structure.

## Conclusion

The evidence supports the skeleton and the data model, not the look. What is verified is the order of sections, the audience routing, the image-first listing, the heritage dates, and the dense regulatory footer. The twelve editorial devices, the type and colour system and the photography brief are inferred practice, and **no competitor was seen to use ruled rows, caption-below images or serif-led typography in a rendered view**. The no-card constraint is therefore a differentiator: incumbents demonstrably use tile and card grids, and Strutt & Parker's claret, monochromatic rebrand suggests the sector is moving toward the restraint this system adopts.

Three gaps should be closed before build. Inspect live CSS and screenshots of Savills, Knight Frank, Strutt & Parker and Carter Jonas for fonts, colours and crop ratios. Retrieve the blocked sites (Hamptons, Cluttons, Jackson-Stops, Chestertons UK, LendInvest). Have a compliance adviser confirm the client's regulatory wording. The most distinctive and least risky moves are the ones the notes found rare: a per-product loan table, a numbered process timeline, and case-study rows with consistent amount, term, LTV, location and date beside real photographs of identifiable UK buildings.
